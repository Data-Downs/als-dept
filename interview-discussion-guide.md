# Discussion Guide — Interviews for *The Diagonal*

**Purpose:** Inform the design work by (1) building a shared picture of what's in flight across the GOV.UK app and its infrastructure, (2) hearing how each person imagines the future fitting together, and (3) collecting a spread of views I can analyse for consensus and divergence — not to follow any single one, but to inspire routes forward.

**Format:** ~45 minutes, semi-structured. I'll ask everyone the same two anchor questions (marked ★) so answers are comparable across interviews. Everything else flexes to the person's vantage point.

**Framing to open with:** *"This feeds a short, design-led prototyping project. I'm talking to a range of people — designers, technologists, senior civil servants — to map what's coming and how people imagine it fitting together. I won't attribute anything to you by name; I'm synthesising across conversations. There are no right answers — I'm after your honest read, including where you're uncertain. Mind if I take notes?"*

---

## At a glance — the 45 minutes

| Time | Section | Aim |
|------|---------|-----|
| 0–4 min | Open & warm-up | Framing, consent, their vantage point |
| 4–19 min | Part 1 — The landscape | What's live, in build, in pipeline |
| 19–39 min | Part 2 — The future | Their imagined future + two key decisions |
| 39–45 min | Part 3 — Close | Biggest opportunity/risk, what I missed, who else |

Adjust the balance to the person (see *Who to lean on what* at the end). With limited time, protect Part 2 — the future views are the scarcest input.

---

## Open & warm-up (0–4 min)

- Deliver the framing above; get consent to take notes.
- ★ *"In a sentence or two — where do you sit, and what's your line of sight into the GOV.UK app and where it's heading?"*

## Part 1 — The landscape: what's in flight (4–19 min)

*Goal: a clear map of what's coming online, what's in development, and what's in the pipeline — across the app, infrastructure, and AI capabilities.*

1. From where you sit, what's the most important thing happening to the GOV.UK app right now?
2. Walk me through it in three buckets: what's **live**, what's **in build**, and what's on the **roadmap but not started**.
   - *Probe:* roughly when does each land? What's blocking the next step?
3. Which of these are you closest to — and for each, what stage is it at, who owns it, and what's it actually for?
   - *Prompt the list so nothing's missed:* One Login / GOV Account · GOV Pay · Notifications / Tell Us Once · Wallet & Credentials · Mailbox / inbox · personalised planning · chat / Dot · the data platforms (UDP, Flex) · agents.
4. On infrastructure specifically — what's the state of the data platforms, UDP and Flex? What do they let us do that we can't today?
5. On AI — where does it sit on the roadmap? How do you see the line between chat/Dot today and something more agentic tomorrow?
   - *Probe (tests a hunch, ask neutrally):* Is personalised planning a separate thing, or part of chat? How would you describe the relationship?
6. When you describe things like chat, mailbox, or planning — do you think of them as **products**, **features**, or **capabilities**? Why that word?
   - *This surfaces how people carve up the space — listen for where the same thing gets called different things.*
7. Where are the seams between all this? What dependencies or overlaps can you see that individual teams probably can't?
8. ★ What do you think is **missing** today that's needed for proactive, joined-up, digital government?

## Part 2 — The future: how it fits together (19–39 min)

*Goal: their imagined future, and a genuine conversation around two decisions I keep coming back to. I won't hold anyone to a prediction — I want the reasoning and the picture.*

Start wide:

9. Picture a citizen in 2030 dealing with government through their phone. What does that actually look like, moment to moment?
   - *Probe:* Are they in an app, in a conversation, or has an agent done it for them? What happened to today's furniture — tabs, an inbox, folders, menus?

Then the **two key decisions** (the heart of Part 2):

10. **One app, or many?** Do you think citizens end up with a single government app, or every department with its own? What forces push it each way?
    - *Probe:* If one app — who owns it, and how do departments plug in? If many — how does a citizen hold it all together?
11. **Opening to third-party agents.** Do you think government will ever open its services to agents beyond its own app — and should it? What would have to be true for that to happen?
    - *Probe (the provision question):* Do you see departments converging on a shared data/API publishing standard, ending up patchy, or each going their own way? What's most likely, and what's best?

Then pull on sequencing and conviction:

12. What's the "penny drop" moment you think is coming — the thing that, once people see it, changes how we'd sequence the roadmap?
13. Where do you have the **most** conviction about the future — and where the **least**?

## Part 3 — Close (39–45 min)

14. Biggest single **opportunity** in this space? Biggest single **risk**?
15. What should this work pay attention to that I haven't asked about?
16. Who else should I be talking to?

Thank them; note you'll share back the synthesis, not individual attributions.

---

## Who to lean on what

- **Designers / design leads** — go deeper on Part 1 capabilities/experience and Part 2 Q9 (the 2030 picture, what dissolves). Lighter on infrastructure detail.
- **Technologists** — go deeper on Part 1 Q4–5 (UDP, Flex, identity, AI) and Part 2 Q11 (publishing standards, feasibility). They'll have the sharpest read on the provision side.
- **Senior civil servants** — protect Part 2 Q10–11 (one-app-vs-many, third-party agents) plus ownership, governance, funding and sequencing. Keep Part 1 to the headline buckets.

## How I'll use it (analysis note)

- Code every transcript against the **two anchor questions** (vantage point; what's missing) and the **two key decisions** (one app vs many; opening to agents) so I can see consensus and divergence at a glance.
- Place each person's future picture on the **commission × provision map** — how they imagine the app being consumed, and how legible they expect services to become.
- Pull recurring **verbatim phrases** (the words people actually use for product/capability/feature) into the shared-terminology work.
- Capture every **"penny drop"** and **dependency** named — these feed the sequencing note.

This is for inspiration and triangulation, not a vote. The aim is a richer set of routes forward, grounded in what the people building this already believe.
