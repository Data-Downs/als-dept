Proposal A — State Machines

Each new interaction type has a unique state machine with type-specific states. Highlighted states are unique to that typology.

Proposal E

Type-Aware Proactivity

Services are now proactively surfaced with type-appropriate framing and priority. Benefits are “suggested,” obligations are “warned,” and information services “inform.”

Priority numbers determine sort order. Lower numbers appear first. Obligations (priority 5) always surface above benefits (10), which surface above information services (40+).

Proposal G

Canonical Data Schemas per Typology

Each typology now defines the characteristic data fields its services typically need. These schemas drive card auto-population, data completeness checks, and pre-fill from citizen profiles.

Typology Key Fields Rationale
£ Benefit employment_status monthly_income savings housing_costs dependants bank_details Income-tested assessment and payment delivery
✓ Entitlement employer_name employment_start_date weekly_earnings qualifying_event_date Employer verification and qualifying period
! Obligation reference_number deadline_date amount_due payment_method Compliance tracking and payment
R Registration event_date event_location participant_name informant_name registration_district Event recording and jurisdiction
A Application full_name date_of_birth address postcode supporting_document Identity confirmation and eligibility
D Document document_type full_name delivery_address delivery_preference Document fulfilment and delivery
§ Legal Process full_legal_name has_solicitor solicitor_name witness_name court_or_registry Legal proceedings and representation

Pre-fill support: Fields with a prefillFrom path (e.g., address.line1) are automatically populated from the citizen’s profile, reducing data entry.
Proposal H

Typology-Level Policy Rules

Policy rules can now be defined at the typology level and inherited by all services of that type. “All benefits require UK residence” is one rule, not 22 copies.

£ Benefit Rules

Locked
typo-benefit-uk-resident
jurisdiction in [England, Wales, Scotland, NI]
Override
typo-benefit-age-minimum
age ≥ 16
Override
typo-benefit-income-declared
income_declared exists
Override
typo-benefit-bank-account
bank_account exists

Inheritance Model

Typology rules merge with service-specific rules. Overridable rules can be replaced by a service with a rule of the same ID.

Example: Universal Credit
Inherits from benefit:
• uk-resident (locked — always applies)
• income-declared ✓
• bank-account ✓

Overrides:
• age-minimum: 16 ➔ 18
Adds:
• low-savings: savings ≤ £16,000
• under-pension-age: age ≤ 66
Typology rules ➔ merge & override ➔ Final ruleset
Architecture Summary

The 5-Stage Pipeline

Every proposal maps to a specific stage in the platform pipeline. Here is where each refinement takes effect.

1
Service Graph
108 services tagged with one of 8 service types (benefit, entitlement, obligation…)
G: Canonical data schemas H: Typology-level policies
2
Type Inference
inferInteractionType() maps serviceType to one of 12 interaction types — now 1:1
A: 1:1 mapping (core fix)
3
State Machine Template
12 templates define unique journey shapes with type-specific states and terminal flows
A: 4 new templates B: Terminal flow overrides
4
Card Resolution
(interactionType + currentState) ➔ type-appropriate UI cards, consent panels, and forms
A: 7 new card definitions C: Type-specific consent framing
5
LLM Instructions
State-specific prompts guide AI agent tone, pace, escalation, and proactive surfacing
D: Type-specific escalation paths E: Type-aware proactivity
Cross-cutting: Proposal F (Studio typology dashboard) provides visibility across all 5 stages. Proposal H (typology-level policies) operates at the graph level but influences policy evaluation at every step.
